# Case File, Part II — The Free City

*The core game loop, re-set in an early-modern city. This is the design to
read and correct before anything is built. Part I (`DESIGN.md`) stays true
for the engine, the table and the tests; everything here is content and
rules on top of it. Where the two disagree, this document wins for the
setting and Part I wins for the machinery.*

Sources are cited in brackets: [Schmidt] Harrington, *The Faithful
Executioner*; [Carolina] the Constitutio Criminalis Carolina, 1532; [Wild]
Moore, *The Thieves' Opera*; [Song Ci] *Collected Cases of Injustice
Rectified*; [Newgate] the Newgate Calendar; [Lewis] *The Assassins*;
[Coquille] the 1455 Dijon trial; [Eco], [Cadfael], [Shardlake], [Kuisl] (The
Hangman's Daughter), [Fingerpost], [Dee], [1793], [Perfume], [Alatriste],
[Hugo], [Apples] (The Three Apples).

## 1. The premise

The year is about 1600. The Free City stands where a river meets the sea:
a walled trading town of forty thousand, ruled by a Council of patrician
families, watched over by a Bishop, fed by guilds, and drained by an
underworld with its own king. The Emperor's law, the Carolina, says what
proof is; the Council decides who is examined; the executioner decides how
they die. Nobody polices the city. The Council has appointed *you* to try.

You are the Council's **Examiner**: the officer who examines crimes, gathers
proof, questions the accused and brings the charge to the Blood Court. In
one person you are police, prosecutor and coroner [Dee]. Every case is a
race against the bell, the Council's patience, and the crowd's appetite.

What the setting changes about the loop, in one sentence each:

- **Proof is a law, not a score.** A charge needs a confession or two
  witnesses. Everything else is *indicia*, and indicia only earn you the
  right to ask harder [Carolina].
- **Confession is the keystone,** and there are honest and dishonest ways
  to get one [Dee, Eco, Kuisl].
- **The verdict is not the end.** After the verdict you *sentence*, and the
  sentence is where mercy and brutality live [Schmidt].
- **You have patrons, and they want answers,** not always true ones
  [Shardlake].
- **The underworld is a state,** with ranks, a court, a king and a language.
  You can break it, bargain with it, or take its throne [Hugo, Coquille,
  Wild].
- **Money is a path, not a failure.** Fees, purses and rewards are how this
  city pays its officers. There is a corrupt way to be good at the job, and
  a greedy way to lose it [Wild].

## 2. Who you are: origins and callings

The current game asks one question at the start (the Calling: Power,
Knowledge or Justice). The city asks two. **Origin** is who you were before
the Council's letter; it sets your cards, one rule that bends for you, and
one door that is shut. **Calling** stays as it is (§19 of Part I) and still
drifts with play.

| Origin | Drawn from | You start with | The rule that bends | The shut door |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Advocate** | Shardlake, Winge [1793] | Focus ×2, a Patron's Commission, the Council's ear | May read the file of any case (see a case's culprit trait clue early, once per case) | Weak: Health ×1; a Wound costs two weeks |
| **The Hangman** | Kuisl, Schmidt | Health ×2, Herbal Lore, the Rack, the keys to the Hole | Examines bodies and wounds alone (Inquest without a physician; Song Ci's tests); may apply the question | **Dishonoured:** cannot testify, hold rank above Bailiff, or enter a patrician house uninvited; witnesses of standing refuse you |
| **The Physician-Monk** | Cadfael, William [Eco] | Focus, Instinct, Herbal Lore, the Abbey's sanctuary | Poisons and plants read at a glance; sanctuary shelters a suspect of yours for forty days | May not carry the sword: no Arrest by force; the Watch must do it, and they are slow |
| **The Watchman** | Cardell [1793], Ma Joong [Dee] | Health ×3, Instinct, a cudgel, the taverns | Beats, chases and doors: Patrol yields more, a hue and cry answers you | Illiterate: Study takes twice as long without a clerk; the Council doubts you |
| **The Clerk of the Court** | Sergeant Hoong [Dee], Lechner [Kuisl] | Focus ×2, Funds ×2, the Council's procedures | Writs, petitions and the Blood Court cost you less; a Sentence you recommend is rarely overruled | No street: no Patrol until you hire a Watchman; the underworld does not know your face |

Origins are five so that the run's opening hour is different five times.
The guided start (Part I) becomes five short scripts, one per origin; each
opens with that person's first morning [1793's torso, Kuisl's council
summons, Cadfael's herb garden, the Advocate's commission].

## 3. The city

Six quarters replace the six districts. Each has a face, a crime it breeds,
and a power that answers for it.

| Quarter | Was | What it is | Breeds | Power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Harbour** | The Docks | Quays, bonded warehouses, foreign sailors, the Customs House | smuggling, drowned men, receiving | the Harbourmaster (Council) |
| **The Market** | Old Market | Stalls, weigh-house, pawnbrokers, the pillory in the square | false weights, coining, cutpurses | the **Muhtasib**, the Market Warden, who fines and flogs without a trial |
| **The Stews** | Neon Row | Taverns, bathhouses, dice-rooms, the spinning-house | brawls, contracts, the Coquille's fronts | the King of Thunes |
| **Patricians' Hill** | Uptown | The great houses, the Council chamber, the Mint | poisonings, forged wills, commissions | the Council |
| **The Warrens** | The Warrens | Tenements against the wall, the Court of Miracles beneath | everything, and no witnesses | the underworld |
| **The Abbey Close** | Canal Street | The Bishop's precinct, the hospital, the scriptorium, sanctuary | heresy accusations, locked-room deaths [Eco] | the Bishop |

The precinct becomes **the Watch-house**: a stone hall by the Market with

## 4. The law of proof

The six clue aspects stay six, renamed so that a period clue can carry them:

| Was | Now | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Forensic | **Body** | What the corpse, the wound, the poison and the ground say [Song Ci]. |
| Testimony | **Word** | What people swear to. |
| Motive | **Motive** | Why anyone would want it done. |
| Opportunity | **Presence** | Who could have been there, then, with the means. |
| Digital | **Writ** | Papers that date themselves: ledgers, letters, seals, the parish register, a forged draft [Newgate]. |
| Financial | **Coin** | Money moving: debts, dowries, pawned goods, a man spending beyond his means [Carolina Art. 43]. |

Charges stop being *weak / reasonable / strong* and become the Carolina's
own ladder:

| Tier | What it takes | What the Court does |
|---|---|---|
| **Indicia** | Any clues at all | Nothing. The Court will not hear it. But indicia let you hold the suspect in the Hole for a week, and *sufficient* indicia (two clues of different aspects, or one Word clue against interest) license *the question* [Carolina Art. 22, 25–44]. |
| **Half proof** | One credible witness (a Word clue from a witness of standing), or one witness plus the suspect's partial admission | The Court convicts of the lesser crime only (theft not burglary, manslaughter not murder), or acquits and lets the crowd decide. |
| **Full proof** | Two credible witnesses agreeing, **or** a confession repeated freely a day after it was given [Carolina Art. 67] | Conviction. Sentence follows (§6). |

**Corroboration** replaces the old contradiction penalty with the
Fingerpost rule: two Word clues from witnesses with *different* motives
establish a fact; two from witnesses with the *same* motive establish
nothing; one witness against their own interest counts double. Every
witness card carries a **motive tag** (protects self, protects kin, wants
the reward, hates the accused, loves the accused, no stake) [Fingerpost].
Reflect shows the tags side by side and says which agree for a reason.

**Confession.** A suspect confronted in Question with clues whose aspects
cover Motive, Presence and one of Body/Writ/Coin confesses honestly
(chance rising with the weight). A suspect confronted with the *question*
(§5) confesses always, and the confession is honest only if they are in
fact guilty; otherwise it is a **False Confession** that reads as full
proof until the Court checks it against the facts [Carolina]: if you have
no Body or Writ clue that agrees with it, the conviction is *wrongful* and
the truth surfaces later (a Loose End with the real culprit's name, and the
Crowd remembers).

## 5. The question, and other ways to be brutal

The city offers force at every step. None of it is forbidden. All of it is
counted.

| Act | Verb | What it gets | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beat a witness** | Question with Health | Word clue at once, from the frightened | Dread +1; that witness never testifies in Court (a beaten witness is not credible) |
| **The question** (torture in the Hole: thumbscrews, strappado, the rack) [Schmidt] | Question with the Rack, only on sufficient indicia | A confession | Dread +2; **Cruelty +1**; the confession may be false; applying it *without* sufficient indicia is a crime the Council can charge you with [Dee] |
| **Search without a writ** | Examine a Suspect's house without a Writ | Body/Presence clue | Suspicion +1 (unchanged) |
| **Plant proof** | Study with a Purse | A Coin or Writ clue that fits | Suspicion +1; a wrongful conviction if used on the innocent |
| **Hue and cry** | Proclamation on a Suspect | The quarter hunts them: a Presence clue and the suspect brought in within the day | If the cry was false (they are innocent), the Council fines you and Standing falls [Statute of Winchester] |
| **Hire the Thief-takers** | Petition: the Thief-takers' Office | A named culprit and the goods back, immediately | Coin; Underworld Debt +1; a hidden chance the culprit is a **frame** [Wild, Macdaniel] |

The counters:

- **Dread** is the city's fear of you. It is a meter beside Pressure. High
  Dread makes witnesses refuse to come forward (fewer Word clues from
  canvassing), quiets the Stews (fewer petty cases), and pleases the
  Council for a while. At 10, the crowd turns [Perfume]: your next
  execution becomes a riot, and the game asks whether you flee or fall.
- **Cruelty** is not a meter. It is a count on the Calling card, like the
  three path scores, and it feeds the endings (§10). It never decreases.
- **Mercy** is its opposite count, earned in §6.

## 6. Sentence: the mercy ladder

Conviction no longer closes a case. It produces a **Condemned** card (the
"poor sinner" [Schmidt]) and opens a new verb, **Sentence**. You recommend;
the Council usually follows (the Clerk origin: always). The ladder is the
Carolina's, and every rung has a price:

| Rung | Who it fits | Effect on the city | Effect on you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pardon** | Anyone, for a reason: youth, penitence, a patron's letter | The criminal walks. Their record stays. Mercy +2. Reformed (50%, more with a Word clue of penitence): their card leaves the game as a citizen. Otherwise they return in the network with *Spared* (they owe you, and the underworld knows it). | The Council frowns (Suspicion +1 if no reason); the crowd is puzzled (Pressure +1 on a high-profile case) |
| **Fine / Restitution** | Theft, fraud, brawls | Coin returns to the victim; a Fund to the Watch-house | Mercy +1; the Muhtasib approves |
| **Pillory** | Cheats, false accusers, petty theft | A day in the square. Pressure −1 (the crowd fed). The criminal's trait becomes public: their next crime is easier to pin. | none |
| **Flogging and banishment** | Repeat theft, vagabonds, a spared man who came back | The criminal leaves the city for ten years. Returns as a **Returned Banished** case if the network wants them (hanging if caught) [Schmidt]. | Dread +1 |
| **Branding / Cropping** | Third theft, perjury | Marked: cannot be a credible witness, cannot be pardoned again; the Coquille takes them (Gang Member at once) | Dread +1, Cruelty +1 |
| **The Sword** | Murder without cruelty, the penitent, the well-born | An "honourable" death. Pressure −2 on a high-profile case. Their gang does not swear vengeance. | Cruelty +1 |
| **The Rope** | Thieves, burglars, receivers | Pressure −2. The gang loses a member. Retaliation +1. | Cruelty +1 |
| **The Wheel / the Fire / the Water** | Murder with cruelty, arson, coining, poisoning, witchcraft [Carolina] | The spectacle: Pressure −3, Dread +2. The underworld remembers your name. Retaliation +2. | Cruelty +2 |
| **Commute** (a second slot) | Any death rung → the Sword, "out of mercy" [Schmidt] | Keeps the Pressure relief, halves the Dread | Mercy +1 |

The Church petitions for the penitent; the Guilds for their members; the
family for anyone. Petitions arrive as cards during the Condemned card's
lifetime (two minutes). Granting one is where a bribe can hide: a
petition with a purse inside (§7).

The ladder is what "be merciful or be brutal" means in play. A merciful
Examiner keeps the city talkative and the underworld resentful in a
different way (spared men become informants, or come back); a brutal one
buys quiet quarters and a Council's gratitude with fear, and finishes
alone.

## 7. The purse: honest, corrupt, or lost

This city pays its officers in fees. That is not corruption; that is the
job. Corruption is when the fee decides the answer.

The **Purse** count (on the Calling card, like Cruelty) rises every time
money changes the truth:

| Temptation | Comes from | What it gives | Purse |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A gratuity** | A victim, after a case closed | 1 Fund | 0 (honest fee) |
| **A purse in a petition** | A family, at Sentence | 2 Funds for the lighter rung | +1 |
| **The Thief-takers' cut** | Recovering goods for a fee without prosecuting [Wild] | 2 Funds per case closed this way | +1, and Underworld Debt |
| **Selling a writ** | A patrician, wanting a rival searched | 3 Funds | +1, Suspicion +1 if the rival is a Council family |
| **The King's tribute** | The underworld, weekly, once a Treaty stands (§8) | Funds every week | +1 per week taken |
| **Blood money** | Convicting for the reward with a frame [Macdaniel] | 3 Funds | +2, and a wrongful conviction |

Two exits from the corrupt road, and the whole design hinges on both
being real:

- **Corrupt and working.** Purse high, cases still closing, wrongful
  convictions few. Suspicion is the risk: the Council's eye. It is
  survivable with Standing (reputation) and a patron. The ending is the
  **Thief-taker General** (§10): the city's most effective officer, and its
  richest, and its worst secret. Jonathan Wild's first fourteen years.
- **Lost to greed.** Purse high *and* the truth stops mattering: three
  wrongful convictions, or two frames, or tribute taken while the network
  grows. The Consumed ending fires early and differently: not a
  breakdown, but the Old Bailey. Someone you hanged has a brother. The
  1718 Act: the Council makes the very thing you do a felony, and your
  own ledger is the two witnesses [Wild].

The line between them is not the amount taken. It is whether you kept
doing the work.

## 8. The underworld: the Coquille and the Court of Miracles

The gangs, syndicate and fronts of Part I keep their machinery and gain a
society. Every criminal record gets a **Coquille role** [Coquille], which is
their trait in the underworld and the skill their crimes show:

| Role | Cant | What they do | The clue they leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crocheteur | lock-picker | burglary | picked lock, no marks |
| Vendengeur | cutpurse | theft in crowds | a slit purse-string |
| Planteur | passer of false coin | coining | clipped coin, silver over lead |
| Beffleur | decoy | lures into rigged games | loaded dice ("gourds") |
| Blanc coulon | the sleeper | robs travellers in inn dormitories | a drugged cup |
| Envoyeur | the sender | murder for hire | a clean wound, a paid man |
| Espieur | the road scout | marks travellers, watches | seen loitering |
| Desrocheur | the stripper | strips a victim of everything | a body without shoes |

Gangs form as now (three At Large make a gang). A gang with four members
and a front swears itself to **the Coquille** (the Syndicate card), and the
Coquille has a court: **the Court of Miracles** in the Warrens, under **the
King of Thunes** [Hugo]. The King is a persistent criminal record with a
name, a face and a history, drawn from the gang lieutenant with the most
crimes when the Coquille forms.

Three stances, and the game does not ask you to pick one; it watches:

**Eradicate.** Break every gang, hang the lieutenants, find the Court and
raid it with a Muster. This is the Crusader's road as now. The Court of
Miracles, raided, scatters into the countryside and comes back as
**highwaymen** (a new case type on the roads outside the walls) unless the
King is taken [Newgate: Turpin, Maclaine]. Breaking the Coquille ends the
game as it does now, and Dread is what it costs, because you cannot raid
the Warrens gently.

**Rule.** Go Undercover into the Court, pass its trial (the bell-hung
dummy: a purse stolen without a sound, which is Instinct against the
King's Presence) [Hugo], and *stay*. From inside you can set the King's
rivals against each other, feed the Watch-house a lieutenant a month, and
finally take the throne. As King of Thunes you run the underworld *and*
the Examiner's office: crimes fall in number and rise in scale; you decide
who is caught. This is Wild's second career, and the **King of Thunes**
ending. It requires Purse ≥ 4 (the Court does not crown the honest) and
Cruelty ≥ 2 (or it does not fear you).

**Treaty.** Meet the King at a front with a Warrant unserved. The
underworld's own court tries its own [Hugo]: a Treaty gives you a suspect
a week from the Court's justice (they hand over the ones they do not
want), tribute if you ask for it (§7), and quiet in the Stews. It costs a
blind eye: two cases a month arrive already **Closed by the Court**, with
the Court's culprit, who may be its scapegoat. The Church hates it, the
Council pretends not to know, and the Crusader path scores nothing while
it stands.

**Outside powers.** Two late-game societies, one per calling drift:

- **The Eumenides** [1793]: a charitable brotherhood of patricians whose
  public face grants Standing and whose private one leaves the Harbour a
  torso a season. Their cases never point at them; only a Thread (two
  cases connected) does. Breaking them is the Master Detective's late
  case, and the Council will not thank you.
- **The Order of the Mountain** [Lewis]: fida'is in the city under cover
  as monks and servants, killing named men in daylight, leaving a dagger
  on a pillow as a warning first. They cannot be broken, only bargained
  with or endured, and any faction can accuse another of hiring them. They
  are what a Commissioner meets on the way to the Chair.

## 9. Patrons, commissions and the crowd

Three powers give you work and want particular answers [Shardlake]. A
**Commission** is a case card with a **Desired Verdict** written on it.

| Patron | Wants | Rewards | If you deliver the truth instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Council** | order, speed, no scandal on the Hill | Funds, rank, writs | Favour −1; a case against a Council family costs Standing, but the Justice path scores it double |
| **The Bishop** | heretics found, the penitent spared, the Abbey's name clean | sanctuary, the Apothecary's help, absolution (Cruelty −1, once per rank) | the Bishop's petitions stop; the Inquisitor arrives (§9.1) |
| **The Guilds** (through the Muhtasib) | cheats punished in the square, their own members fined not hanged | Coin cases, the Market's witnesses | the Market goes quiet for a week |
| **The Crowd** | spectacle when frightened, mercy when it likes the accused [Perfume] | Pressure falls when fed | Pressure rises; at Dread 10, the riot |

A commission delivered as desired against the facts is a **wrongful
conviction** with a patron's protection: the Loose End still surfaces, but
the patron carries the Suspicion, until the patron falls. Patrons can fall
[Shardlake]: a Council election (every twelve weeks) can replace your
patron, and all Favour with the fallen one becomes Suspicion.

**9.1 The Inquisitor** [Eco, Alatriste]. When a case is stamped
*heresy* or *witchcraft* and stays open two weeks, or when the Bishop's
Favour is low, the Inquisitor arrives as a verb you do not control. He
takes the Condemned before you can sentence, and the question is applied
whether you would or not. He is the design's answer to "the accused is
guilty because he was accused": the only defence is proof so full he
cannot ignore it, or getting the suspect into sanctuary first (the
Physician-Monk's door).
